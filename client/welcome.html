why Kith exists

Kids are going to talk to AI.
So we built one that's on their side.

Most AI apps weren't made for young people: they run on servers you'll never see, keep conversations you'll never read, and are designed to keep anyone hooked. Kith started with one parent asking, "what if I built my teenager one of their own?"

  • 🫧

    Theirs

    They name it, choose its personality and voice, and it remembers what matters to them: the test on Thursday, the story they're writing, the character they're drawing.

  • 🏡

    Yours to run

    It lives on your own home server, behind your own sign-in, on your own Claude API key with a spending limit you choose. Chats are deleted after 30 days.

  • 🤝

    Honest

    Everyday chats stay private. If something's worrying, Kith tells your child what it will share with you, and why, before it does. No secret reports, ever.

safety, built in, not bolted on

Safety sits under the personality, and nothing changes it.

  • Every message is checked, both ways, by a separate safety check. If it can't run, the reply waits.
  • Personal details are removed before anything leaves your home, and it never asks for them.
  • No romance, nothing sexual, no money, no meet-ups. Ever.
  • "Grown-up?" on every screen: one tap to message you, Childline, Samaritans or 999.
  • Told first, then shared. Your child sees the exact words before you get them.
  • Designed with the UK's Age Appropriate Design Code in mind.

kith parent

Everything a grown-up needs. None of the snooping.

You see

  • Reports when something matters: right now, today, or just so you know
  • A weekly summary at topic level, the same one your child sees
  • Spending against a weekly allowance and a hard monthly limit
  • Every tool and connection it used, and everything on its desk

You never see

  • Their everyday chats
  • Their memories, which are theirs to read and delete

You decide

Budgets, quiet hours, helplines, which websites its browser may visit, the safeguards document, skills, and connections to Google, your calendar or your smart home, each one look-only or allowed to act.

get kith for your family

Free, open source, and yours.

Kith is for families comfortable running a small home server. Setup takes an afternoon, and a guided script keeps your keys hidden as you type them.

  1. 1

    A home server

    Any always-on computer that runs Docker. Light mode is happy on an old Mac mini.

  2. 2

    Your own Claude key

    From your own Anthropic organisation, with a monthly limit. Defaults are built for heavy daily use inside $50 a month.

  3. 3

    A family sign-in

    A sign-in provider such as Pocket ID, so only your family gets in.

Questions parents ask

Will it replace their friends?
No. It always says it's an AI, never guilt-trips them into staying, and nudges them back to real friends and family.
Can I read their chats?
No, and that's on purpose. You get reports when something matters and a weekly summary you both see.
What does it cost?
Kith is free. You pay Anthropic for what it uses, capped by limits you set; everyday use fits inside $50 a month.
Are chats used to train AI?
No. Kith uses the Claude API under commercial terms, and chats stay on your server for 30 days at most.
